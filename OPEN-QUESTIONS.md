The project's rule applies throughout: where the law defines a boundary that no public dataset carries, the map says so rather than inventing geometry. A plausible polygon that turns out to be wrong is worse than an honest marker. Where a rule is ambiguous, the map draws the reading that closes more water and the flag says which reading was drawn.

## Flagged map features

The groups below sort the 111 flags by why the geometry differs from the law. Each line gives the zone id, the display name, its map kind (`closed`, `warn`, `open` or `line`), the file it lives in, the citations it joins to, and the flag text. Where several features in a group share identical flag text, the text is printed once at the head of the group as "shared text A" (or B) and each feature refers to it. ## Questions that need a human, ranked

Ranked by how much water, or how many dives, the answer moves. Numbers are kept from the 30 September list so that references in the data and the research notes still match; resolved questions have moved to the lists at the end of this section. Each open question carries its status after the 7 October pass: PARTLY RESOLVED or NEEDS A PERSON.

### Open

- **1. Key West and Great White Heron national wildlife refuges: open or closed?** PARTLY RESOLVED.
  50 C.F.R. § 27.21 bars taking any animal on a refuge unless part 32 opens it. Re-checked 7 Oct 2026: § 32.28 was last amended 30 Oct 2023, neither refuge appears in it, and the 2026 to 2027 station-specific rule (91 FR 56290; correction 91 FR 60900) has no Florida text. The Fish and Wildlife Service's own pages, read the same day, still say "Sport and commercial fishing in accordance with state fishing laws" and that refuge waters are open to fishing, snorkeling and diving, and that the submerged lands are managed by the State of Florida under the 1992 agreement. Both refuges stay `warn` with the conflict stated (`nwr-kew`, `nwr-gwh`). Ask the Florida Keys National Wildlife Refuges Complex, Big Pine Key (305-872-2239, number not verified): "Do refuge officers treat spearfishing under Florida law as permitted in Key West and Great White Heron NWR waters, given that 50 CFR part 32 does not list either refuge as open to sport fishing?"

- **2. Volusia County: what are the "inland salt waters"?** NEEDS A PERSON.
  R. 68B-3.008 never defines them and FWC publishes no boundary (`vol`). Ask FWC's Division of Law Enforcement how the line is enforced. Until then assume the whole Halifax River and Mosquito Lagoon system.

- **3. Federal mackerel gear: may a recreational diver spear king mackerel or Atlantic Spanish mackerel in federal waters?** PARTLY RESOLVED.
  Both texts were read in their current eCFR versions on 7 Oct 2026. § 600.725(v) lists spear for the coastal migratory pelagics recreational fishery, but its opening says the list does not "alter or supersede" other regulations in the chapter and that gear is prohibited "when such fishing gear is prohibited or restricted by regulation under an FMP". § 622.375(a)(1) leaves spear off "the only fishing gears" for directed fisheries for Atlantic king, Gulf king and Atlantic Spanish mackerel, so the conservative reading stands; the stated consequence in (b)(3) is the bag limit. "Directed fisheries" is still undefined. Ask NOAA Fisheries Southeast Regional Office, Sustainable Fisheries Division (727-824-5305, number not verified): "Does NMFS treat a recreational diver spearing king mackerel or Atlantic Spanish mackerel in the EEZ as a directed fishery using unauthorized gear, and is the bag limit the only consequence?"

- **4. Is a dive charter a "vessel for hire" under r. 68B-14.009(4)?** NEEDS A PERSON.
  The adopted text (effective 1 Sep 2026) was read on 7 Oct 2026: the species list is unchanged, and the new Atlantic For-Hire Reef Fish Registry for 2026 to 2028 reaches the 13 species "within or without Florida Waters of the Atlantic Ocean". The text does not say whether a boat that carries paying spearfishers is covered. FWC (850-487-0554, number not verified).

- **5. Venice § 46-114: how far is "near"?** NEEDS A PERSON.
  "It shall be unlawful for any person to engage or attempt to engage in spear fishing within or near the territorial waters of the city." The whole city polygon is drawn (`loc-venice`) and Venice Inlet treated as closed. Ask the Venice city clerk or the police marine unit what distance they apply.

- **6. Treasure Island: § 22-2 "designated as permissible", and the width of § 58-34.** NEEDS A PERSON.
  A whole-code search on 7 Oct 2026 found no designated spearfishing area, and resolutions are not on Municode. § 22-2 bans discharging spring, gas or mechanically powered projectile weapons citywide except "to a person spear fishing in any area where such activity has been designated as permissible either by the city, the state or the federal government" (`loc-ti222`); § 58-34 gives a 1,500-ft distance out from two points and no lateral limit (`ti58`). Ask City Clerk Celine Kidwell, (727) 547-4575 ext. 228 (from the votepinellas.gov municipal clerks list): "Has the city designated any area as permissible for spearfishing under § 22-2(b), and does it treat water where state law allows spearfishing as designated? What lateral width does it apply to the § 58-34 extensions?"

- **7. Sunny Isles Beach § 108-4: a standing rule or a dangerous-conditions rule?** PARTLY RESOLVED.
  The whole section was read on 7 Oct 2026. Every other sentence carries a dangerous-conditions trigger; the last ("Except in designated areas, no person shall fish in the Atlantic Ocean by use of hook and line, seine, trap, spear gig or other device.") has none, while the article heading and purpose clause point to dangerous conditions only. No designated area exists in the code. Drawn as standing (`loc-sib`). Ask the City Clerk, 305.792.1703, cityclerk@sibfl.net (from the city's departments page): "Is the last sentence of § 108-4 enforced at all times, and has the city designated any area where fishing, including spearfishing, is allowed in the Atlantic?"

- **8. Lee County § 20-25: how far do "adjacent littoral waters" run?** NEEDS A PERSON.
  County parks, defined to include beaches, piers and adjacent littoral waters, ban fishing with spears at all times. Only the Lynn Hall pier is drawn (`loc-lee-lynnhall`). Ask Lee County Parks and Recreation for the seaward extent, and for a list of the beach parks it applies to.

- **9. Hallandale Beach § 16-23: does the "public beach" include the water?** PARTLY RESOLVED.

## Unsolved by design: the fresh/salt line

This is a gap in the law, not in the data, and it is not going to close.

Florida's test for salt water is a taste test: water that has become "unpalatable because of the saline content", Fla. Stat. § 379.101(33). There is no salinity threshold in fisheries law, no per-river list and no FWC GIS layer. Only the Steinhatchee River has a designated boundary. The one numeric salinity line in Florida law, 1,500 mg/L chloride in Fla. Admin. Code r. 62-302.200(30), belongs to DEP water-quality classification, not to FWC licensing, and FDEP's surface-water GIS carries only the specially classified named waters, not a statewide marine/fresh split. That was checked directly: the layer exists and does not contain what would be needed. R. 62-302.200 was amended on 25 November 2025 without changing (30), and § 379.101 was last amended in 2025 without changing (33).

**What the map does instead.** Every bridge, pier and ramp carries a coastal, tidal or inland classification inferred by nearest-neighbour vote from FWC's own labelled ramps (95.1 per cent leave-one-out agreement). The "Inland waters" toggle hides everything classified inland in one click (4,958 bridges and 239 piers), so the interior of the state can be dismissed wholesale. The classification is a planning heuristic with no legal force, and every popup says so.

**Why that is enough in practice.** The question only bites in brackish water, where visibility is usually too poor to hunt. Where it does bite, carry both licences and treat the stricter regime as controlling: the freshwater side is where the speargun ban lives.
