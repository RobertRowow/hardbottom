About Bottom Truth

A map of Florida waters researched as closed or restricted to spearfishing, with the law behind each closure.

Not legal advice. Not official. Not for navigation.

This map is an informational reference and is not a substitute for the advice of an attorney. It shows areas researched as closed or restricted. Unshaded water is not shown as open: many rules, including the 100 yd beach buffer on bays, lagoons and beaches outside the state's monument chains and the buffers around piers missing from the FWC inventory, are not drawn. Boundaries are approximate drawings of rules written in words, not surveyed legal lines. Regulations change. Verify with FWC, NOAA and the local county before you dive, and obey posted signs.

What the map shows

  • Closures and restrictions drawn from state statutes and rules, federal regulations, Coast Guard and military zones, the Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary, national parks and wildlife refuges, manatee protection zones and county and city ordinances.
  • The citation behind every zone. The Law button opens each rule with its verbatim text, whether that text was checked word for word at the official source, and a link to the source.
  • Structure for planning: artificial reefs, charted wrecks and obstructions, natural hardbottom, depth contours, charted bottom type, curated dive spots and boat ramps.
  • Research coverage. A layer shows how thoroughly each area has been researched, so that a blank area is not mistaken for open water.

How boundaries are drawn

Where a rule gives coordinates, they are drawn as written. Where a rule defines a boundary only in words, it is drawn from the nearest published geometry that contains it and flagged, or it is shown as a marker. Buffers are drawn wider than the rule (125 yd for a 100 yd rule, 150 ft for a 100 ft rule), and where a rule is ambiguous the reading that closes more water is drawn. Open questions, including every zone that could not be drawn exactly, are listed in the open questions file.

Report an error or a sign, or send a suggestion

If the map disagrees with a posted sign or the law, please report it with the location, what the map shows, what the sign or law says, and a photo or link if you have one. Tips and suggestions are welcome the same way.

  • Email contact@bottomtruth.com. No account is needed. Emails are read by the author and are not published.
  • Open an issue on GitHub (a free GitHub account is needed; issues are public).

Each report is checked against the primary source before the map changes, and every correction is listed in the changelog.

Support

Bottom Truth is free and has no ads, and the legal layers will stay free. The most useful help is a correction from someone who dives the water in question. Gifts from speargun and dive gear makers, and from groups that campaign for or against closures, are not accepted, so that no reader has reason to ask whether a line is drawn where it is for any reason other than the law.

Data sources

All legal text comes from primary sources: Online Sunshine for Florida statutes, FLRules for the Florida Administrative Code, eCFR for federal regulations, and Municode or the county or city's own code site for local ordinances. Map data comes from NOAA, the USGS, FWC, FDEP, USFWS, the National Park Service, BOEM, the Census Bureau and the Federal Highway Administration; the optional street base map is OpenStreetMap, and 39 stand-in points on the jetty layer are OpenStreetMap positions. The full list, with record counts and terms, is in DATA-SOURCES.md.

Agency names are listed as sources only. Bottom Truth is not affiliated with or endorsed by any government agency.
